Multi-lingual Bidirectional Sign Language Translation System for Real-Time Communication Between Hearing and Hearing-Impaired Communities in Sri Lanka Ongoing
Abstract
“Abstract The communication barrier between hearing-impaired individuals and the general public remains a critical challenge in Sri Lanka, severely impacting access to essential services such as education, healthcare, and employment. Existing assistive technologies are largely inadequate for this context, as they typically support only one-way translation, recognize isolated gestures rather than continuous signing, and fail to accommodate Sri Lanka's complex multilingual landscape of Sinhala, Tamil, and English. To bridge this gap, this study proposes an AI-driven, bidirectional sign language translation framework tailored specifically for Sri Lankan Sign Language (SLSL). The system integrates computer vision and deep learning models to accurately track and interpret continuous sign gestures, alongside natural language processing (NLP) and speech processing technologies to manage language translation. Designed to handle multiple input modalities, the platform accepts sign language, voice commands, and typed text, instantly generating the corresponding output via visual sign language, synthesized audio, or written text across all three local languages. By providing real-time, two-way translation in diverse environments, this research aims to foster greater social inclusion and equal communication opportunities for the deaf and hard-of-hearing community. Ultimately, the outcomes of this study will provide a robust technical foundation for future advancements in multilingual assistive technologies. Key Words: Sign Language Translation, Sri Lankan Sign Language (SLSL), Bidirectional Communication, Multilingual Processing, Assistive Technology, Deep Learning.
